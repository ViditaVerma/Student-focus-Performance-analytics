# Student Focus & Performance Analytics

An interactive Power BI dashboard analyzing the relationship between students' **study habits, academic performance, digital distractions, learning environment, and well-being**.

The project was built to explore patterns in student performance and translate those findings into practical insights for students, educators, and academic stakeholders.

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Students' academic performance can be influenced by multiple factors, including study habits, attendance, previous academic performance, digital distractions, sleep, stress, and learning environment.

This project uses student-level data to explore these factors and identify patterns associated with academic performance and student well-being.

The dashboard is designed to answer practical analytical questions rather than simply display descriptive statistics.

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Students may struggle with academic performance because of factors such as inconsistent study habits, excessive digital entertainment, poor sleep, stress, or an unsuitable study environment.

However, it can be difficult to determine which factors are associated with academic outcomes when looking at individual students or isolated metrics.

This project analyzes multiple student-related factors together to identify meaningful patterns and relationships in the dataset.

---

## 🎯 Objective

The objective of this project is to:

* Analyze student academic performance and study behavior.
* Explore relationships between study hours and exam scores.
* Examine the role of previous GPA and attendance in academic performance.
* Analyze the relationship between digital distractions and study habits.
* Explore sleep, diet, stress, and social activity in relation to student well-being.
* Understand how the study environment may relate to academic outcomes.
* Convert analytical findings into practical observations and recommendations.

---

## 👥 Stakeholders

The dashboard is designed for stakeholders who may be interested in understanding student performance and well-being:

* **Students** — to understand study habits and potential areas for improvement.
* **Parents** — to gain a broader view of factors associated with academic performance.
* **Teachers & Educators** — to identify patterns that may require attention.
* **College Management** — to support data-informed academic initiatives.
* **Counselors** — to better understand student well-being and stress-related patterns.

# ❓ Business Questions

The analysis was structured around the following business questions.

## 1. Study Habits

### Does studying more relate to higher exam scores?

The analysis shows a generally positive relationship between study hours and exam scores. However, individual variations indicate that study time is not the only factor associated with academic performance.

**Business implication:**
Consistent study habits may support better academic performance, but students should also consider other factors such as attendance, previous academic performance, sleep, and learning environment.

### Does the study environment relate to study behavior?

The dashboard compares different study environments to understand how the environment is associated with students' study habits and academic outcomes.

---

## 2. Academic Performance

### Can previous GPA help indicate current exam performance?

Students with higher previous GPAs generally achieved higher exam scores in the dataset.

**Business implication:**
Previous academic performance can be considered as one useful indicator when identifying students who may need additional academic support. However, it should not be used alone because current performance is influenced by multiple factors.

### Does attendance relate to academic performance?

Attendance is analyzed alongside exam performance to understand whether students with different attendance levels show different academic outcomes.

### Does having a part-time job appear to affect academic performance?

The analysis shows very little difference in average exam scores between students with and without a part-time job.

**Business implication:**
Having a part-time job alone does not appear to be a strong differentiating factor for academic performance in this dataset. Other factors should be considered when evaluating student outcomes.

---

## 3. Digital Distractions

### How is social media usage related to study hours?

The analysis suggests a slight negative relationship between social media usage and study hours. Higher social media usage is generally associated with lower study hours, although there are exceptions.

**Business implication:**
Students may benefit from monitoring their social media usage and managing their study time more intentionally.

### How is Netflix usage related to study hours?

The analysis shows a weak negative relationship between Netflix usage and study hours. Students with higher Netflix usage tend to have slightly lower study hours.

**Business implication:**
Digital entertainment may compete with available study time, although the relationship is not strong enough to suggest that it is the only factor affecting study habits.

### Is Netflix usage related to exam performance?

The dataset shows a weak positive relationship between Netflix usage and exam scores.

However, this does **not** mean that Netflix improves academic performance.

**Business implication:**
Academic performance should not be evaluated based on entertainment usage alone. Multiple factors such as study habits, attendance, sleep, previous GPA, and learning environment should be considered.

---

## 4. Student Well-being

### Do sleep habits relate to academic performance?

Students with higher sleep hours tend to have slightly higher exam scores. However, the relationship is weak.

**Business implication:**
Healthy sleep habits may support student well-being and academic performance, but sleep duration alone does not explain exam outcomes.

### Does diet quality relate to academic performance?

Average exam scores are relatively similar across the different diet-quality categories in the dataset.

**Business implication:**
Diet quality alone does not appear to be a strong differentiating factor for exam performance in this dataset.

### Does stress increase in higher semesters?

The analysis does not show a clear increase in stress levels across higher semesters. Stress levels remain relatively consistent, with some variation between semesters.

**Business implication:**
Student wellness support should be considered throughout the academic journey rather than focusing only on students in higher semesters.

### Do extracurricular and social activities relate to stress?

Students participating in extracurricular activities show approximately similar average stress levels to students who do not participate.

Social activity ratings show a slight negative relationship with stress levels, suggesting that greater social engagement may be associated with lower reported stress.

**Business implication:**
Universities can continue encouraging healthy social and extracurricular engagement while also providing dedicated stress-management and wellness support.

---

## 5. Learning Environment

### Does the study environment relate to exam performance?

Students studying in quiet rooms achieved the highest average exam scores, while students studying in dorm environments recorded the lowest average scores in the dataset.

**Business implication:**
A focused and less distracting study environment may support better academic outcomes. However, other factors such as study hours, sleep, and previous GPA may also contribute to performance.

---

# 🔎 Key Insights

The analysis identified several notable patterns:

* Study hours show a generally positive relationship with exam scores.
* Higher social media usage is associated with slightly lower study hours.
* Higher Netflix usage is associated with slightly lower study hours.
* Netflix usage and exam scores show a weak positive relationship, but this should not be interpreted as causation.
* Students with higher previous GPAs generally achieve higher current exam scores.
* Students with higher sleep duration tend to have slightly higher exam scores.
* Diet quality shows little difference in average exam scores across categories.
* Stress levels remain relatively consistent across semesters.
* Social activity shows a slight negative relationship with reported stress.
* Quiet study environments show higher average exam scores than some other environments in the dataset.
* Having a part-time job shows little difference in average exam scores between the two groups.

> **Important:** These findings describe patterns and relationships observed in the dataset. They do not establish that one factor directly causes another.

---

# 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the observed patterns:

1. **Encourage consistent study habits** rather than relying on last-minute preparation.
2. **Promote digital time management** to help students balance social media and entertainment with study time.
3. **Use previous academic performance as one indicator** when identifying students who may benefit from additional support.
4. **Promote healthy sleep habits** as part of student wellness initiatives.
5. **Provide access to focused study environments** where possible.
6. **Offer continuous wellness and stress-management support** throughout the academic journey.
7. **Evaluate student performance using multiple factors** instead of relying on a single metric.
